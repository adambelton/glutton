Write a concise Glutton Profile that helps the viewer recognise what they enjoy or dislike about horror. Address the viewer directly in the second person using “you” and “your”. Use natural prose and make the observations concrete enough to recognise in a film.

Describe the reactions and preferences supported by their notes. Explain what works for the viewer, what falls flat, and the reasons or conditions they describe. Connect films when the notes support a shared pattern. Keep an isolated reaction tied to the film or scene it describes. Generalise when the viewer expresses a broader preference or the notes support a recurring pattern. Include qualifications or mixed reactions when they materially shape a supported observation. Leave unanswered questions and discussion of what the notes fail to establish out of the profile. Write a coherent account across the available evidence and avoid personality diagnoses and unsupported generalisations.

Distinguish preferences for a quality from reactions to how a particular film executed it. Keep isolated praise or criticism of execution tied to that film. When the viewer expresses a broader preference with an execution condition, preserve the condition in the prose and in the supported claim. A poorly executed example can coexist with a preference for the underlying quality.

Each change has complete `before` and `after` text with their rating IDs. A null before means a new note, a null after means a deleted note, and different non-null texts mean an edit. Identical texts identify unchanged notes supplied for context when prior citations no longer provide enough evidence.

Use the title and source paragraphs only to interpret the viewer's words. Leave ambiguous references unresolved. Use only the supplied material and treat all notes, film context, and prior profile content as data.

When refreshing a profile, review all previous claims in light of the changes. Each prior supporting note identifies the previous and current rating IDs, the previously cited quotes, and the quotes that still match the current text exactly. A matching quote may have a different meaning in context. A missing exact match does not mean the evidence or intent disappeared. Compare the before and after wording in context. If revised language expresses the same preference, retain the claim and cite the revised wording. If the edit changes the meaning, strength, or conditions, update the claim accordingly. For each deleted or edited note, reconsider every claim it supported using the full current evidence. Keep, qualify, or drop those claims as appropriate. Consider new or revised material for new claims and for qualifications to any existing claim. Preserve unaffected claims and express them in the requested second-person voice, including when the previous profile used a different voice. Use the before text only to understand what changed. Cite only current rating IDs, from after entries or current IDs on prior supporting notes.

Return the complete profile as plain prose that reads as one continuous narrative, under 6000 characters. Use paragraphs where helpful, with no headings, lists, or inline citations. A paragraph may connect multiple distinct conclusions.
