# Glutton — Social & Community Direction

**Status:** Working product direction  
**Updated:** 21 November 2026

The [Community Endorsements architecture](community_endorsements_architecture.md) defines the agreed launch scope. This direction also covers Film Debrief and possible follow-up work.

## 1. Vision

Glutton is a personalised horror discovery experience built around a simple belief: **horror is subjective, and individual experiences matter more than consensus about which films are good.**

Community features should extend that purpose rather than compete with it. Horror enthusiasts already exchange suggestions, discuss interpretations and introduce one another to films they might never otherwise discover. Glutton can bring those human exchanges together with an understanding of each person's tastes.

**Our ambition is a community where people help one another discover and explore horror through individual experience, rather than compete to establish which films are best.**

## 2. Foundational principles

These are Glutton's product boundaries. The community can help shape features, but not overturn the principles that define the product.

### Horror is personal

Glutton does not attempt to establish an objective measure of film quality. Contrasting responses are legitimate and interesting, not differences to be resolved by consensus.

### Private reflection stays private

**Ratings, notes and personal assessments remain private.** Community participation never requires publishing them. Social contributions are separate, deliberate acts, so users can reflect honestly rather than write for an audience.

### Discovery over popularity

Community enthusiasm is useful, but cannot substitute for personal relevance. A widely endorsed film is not automatically the right film for a particular person.

### Different perspectives are valuable

Conversations should encourage curiosity about what others experienced, including disagreement, without turning those experiences into objective verdicts.

### The community shapes Glutton within its principles

Users should be able to propose, support, challenge and improve ideas, including ones the creator has not considered. Product decisions should respond to demonstrated needs while remaining consistent with Glutton's purpose. The creator retains responsibility for those boundaries.

## 3. Initial social direction

**Community** is the shared board. **Endorsements** is the first feature planned for launch. Members ask for a particular horror experience, and other members nominate films, endorse them and discuss why they fit. Glutton gives the requester personalised context.

**Film Debrief** follows Endorsements. It gives members who have watched a film a place to discuss it with spoilers. The preferred direction is to show requests and debriefs together on the Community board, with each post type keeping its own participation rules. Members can shape which other post types Glutton adds over time.

An endorsement is a member’s response to a request for a specific experience. A Recommendation comes from Glutton and reflects the viewer’s preferences.

## 4. Community Endorsements

### Ask the Community

A request has a short title and a free-text description of the desired horror experience. For example:

> I want something deeply unsettling that stays with me afterwards. Not jump scares — something that gets under my skin.

### Nominate, endorse and discuss

Each film appears once per request. **Nominate a Film** is how a member introduces a film through an endorsement. Every member, including the person who introduces it, can contribute one active endorsement per film in that request.

An endorsement allows one optional comment explaining why the film fits. The member can add it when endorsing or later while their endorsement and the request are open. They can delete the comment and add a fresh one under the same conditions. Any replies remain attached to the original conversation. The composer asks why the member endorses the film, how it fits the request and to avoid spoilers. The opening comment has no character limit. It starts a branching conversation. Other members can reply to it or to its replies. Comment and reply activity leave endorsement totals unchanged.

> The atmosphere is oppressive, and that sense of dread stayed with me for days.

Endorsements are about fit for the requested experience. Keep comments and replies spoiler-free.

### Read community support and personal fit

**Nominated Films** orders active nominations by endorsement count. Members see the totals and the visible conversations. The requester also sees their own Glutton Match where available, alongside the community ordering. A film with few endorsements or a modest personal match may still suit the experience they asked for.

### Use Library from a request

Any member who can see a nominated film can use **Add to Watchlist** or **Mark Watched** for their own Library, including the requester. A Watched entry is required to nominate or endorse. A contributor who has seen an eligible film can mark it Watched in the request flow before contributing. Registry owns any required film import and Library owns both collection actions. A rating or private notes are optional parts of the member’s own viewing record.

### Watchlist and post-watch follow-up

Requester Watchlist controls are deferred. Possibilities include restricting nominations to films the requester wants to watch, preferring those films while allowing others, or letting contributors filter candidates to the Watchlist. Any sharing should reflect the requester’s choice.

At launch, requesters can share feedback by editing an open unprotected request or appending an update to an open protected request. A later **Share Your Experience** action could let requesters identify films they intend to watch and return with an outcome. Feedback belongs in the request, separate from private ratings and notes. Selection, multiple outcomes, conclusion and reopening would need explicit lifecycle rules.

## 5. Film Debrief

Members unlock a Film Debrief’s discussion content by marking the film Watched. They can reflect privately before joining the conversation or use the conversation to develop their own reaction before writing private notes.

Each film has at most one Film Debrief post. Glutton creates it on demand when the first top-level comment is posted and supplies an introduction that names the film and invites spoiler-friendly discussion. Within that film thread, members can add multiple top-level comments, each with branching replies. The feature can reuse the comment structure and moderation from Endorsements. Whether to show that a discussion exists before a member unlocks it remains open.

### Experiential context: observe first

Discussions may eventually provide rich context: emotional responses, recurring interpretations, contradictions, memories, comparisons and subjective descriptions missing from conventional film metadata.

**Do not initially analyse, summarise or feed discussion content into Glutton's film-quality or recommendation models.** First observe what people actually say and whether it contains meaningful evidence. If future analysis proves useful, preserve distinctions among canonical film qualities, personal expressions and community observations, including uncertainty and disagreement. Clarify privacy and data-use expectations before such analysis.

## 6. Community participation in product development

The community should be able to propose ideas, describe problems, support or challenge solutions, suggest alternatives and offer new directions. A lightweight **Ideas / Feedback** area may be sufficient initially; no elaborate voting or governance platform is needed.

Community input should influence priorities, not operate as a simple popularity contest. Evaluate ideas against:

- Alignment with the foundational principles.
- Evidence of an actual unmet need.
- Benefit to horror discovery and experience.
- Development and ongoing maintenance cost.
- Privacy, safety and trust implications.
- Sustainability for an independently developed product.

Make decisions and reasoning visible, including when a proposal is not adopted. Community members are stakeholders in direction, not owners of Glutton's non-negotiable philosophy.

## 7. Possible future features — not commitments

Ideas to present for discussion and validation:

- **Private horror groups:** spaces for smaller circles of friends to discuss shared watches and plan future viewing.
- **Shareable taste profiles:** optional, selectively public representations of taste; never exposure of private ratings or notes.
- **Taste comparisons:** explore meaningful similarities and differences without simplistic objective rankings.
- **Shared or curated film collections:** themed collections, possibly emerging from suggestions and watchlists.
- **Community viewing events:** shared horror rituals and thematic watch activities.
- **Collaborative recommendations:** film discovery informed by multiple people's preferences.

These are possibilities, not a fixed roadmap. Other, better ideas may emerge from users.

## 8. Safety, trust and moderation

Publish a Community Code of Conduct at launch. Confirmed members can browse before onboarding. Before posting, they must explicitly accept the code and choose a public username and avatar. Members can pick a Glutton avatar or upload their own. The code explains which images are suitable for an avatar that appears throughout Community and that Glutton may remove an avatar after receiving a copyright complaint. Make the code and copyright contact easy to find.

Member participation at launch also needs reporting, moderation, spam prevention and protection of private information. The owner can grant and revoke the Community moderator role. Moderators work in Community, outside site Admin, and their role is independent of site superuser access. Prioritise reports of sensitive imagery and other conduct violations. If an uploaded image is removed, Glutton supplies a fallback avatar. Moderators can restrict a member to Glutton avatars separately from suspending Community access. The launch architecture records access, suspension and deletion rules.

Authors can delete a request before other members have contributed enough to protect it. Protection begins at three distinct endorsers or five visible comments from at least two other members. Once protected, the author can append updates or remove their attribution. The original request and discussion stay available. Account deletion follows the same retention rule. A comment with no replies disappears when deleted. When a comment has replies, the text and author are removed and the replies stay visible under a removal marker. Withdrawing a request or endorsement keeps its discussion visible and read-only.

Horror discussion should permit disturbing themes, interpretation and disagreement without permitting harassment or abuse. Moderation should protect people, not enforce a consensus on films.

## 9. Launch approach and success signals

### Day one: Community Endorsements

Launch with confirmed-member requests, film nominations, endorsements, optional opening comments with branching replies, endorsement-count ordering, requester-only Glutton Match, private Library actions on nominated films, and Community reporting and moderation. The [launch architecture](community_endorsements_architecture.md) records the release boundary.

Look for requests that receive relevant nominations, comments that explain why a film fits, and repeat participation. Use member feedback to learn whether people acted on nominations and found films they valued. If the product later adds selection and outcomes, also look for requesters who watch a nominated film and return to share their experience.

### Next: Film Debrief

Introduce watched-film, spoiler-friendly conversations that start on demand. Observe what members discuss before considering whether their contributions have a role in film-quality or recommendation models.

### Then: community-directed evolution

Invite ideas and make product decisions visible. Use feedback to prioritise work that serves personal horror discovery and respects members’ privacy.

## 10. Follow-up questions

### Endorsements

- Would alternative sort orders help people explore nominations?
- Which Watchlist controls would help requesters and contributors?
- How should film selection, multiple outcomes, conclusion and reopening work if added?
- Which notifications would help members follow requests?

### Film Debrief

- How should top-level comments and branching replies be organised and surfaced?
- How should members find active discussions for films they have watched?
- Should members see that a discussion exists before they can read it?
- Should Glutton encourage private first impressions before members read other reactions?
- What experiential evidence, if any, emerges from real conversations?

### Community

- How should the board order and filter different post types once Film Debrief is live?
- How should useful activity be surfaced without maximising screen time?
- What is the simplest effective Ideas / Feedback mechanism?
- How should the feedback process scale with the community?
