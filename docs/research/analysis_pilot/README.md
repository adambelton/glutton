## Outcome

Owner review found revision 1 too minimal and conclusion-focused. The five dossiers now put a seven-part chronological narrative and six contextualised key moments before the dimension summaries. The narrative explains decisions and consequences; moments separate reported action, response, inference and missing evidence. Source registers now contain 32 sources across 33 distinct URLs (one Filmsite account spans two pages).

These are source-based drafts, not accounts of direct viewing. No independent curator has reviewed them. The exercise demonstrates a way to organise evidence and useful distinctions; it does not establish recommendation accuracy, production-ready source access, or complete film coverage.

1. Read task 025 requirements and the existing briefs/scoring design.
2. Target the five original features and theatrical narratives. Check identity against the film source sets; retain unresolved cut differences rather than asserting an inspected release.
3. Read accessible reviews and factual plot accounts, seeking complementary perspectives and scene-specific responses. The initial registers contained 19 URLs; revision 2 expands to 33 URLs representing 32 sources. All were retrieved on 27 October 2026. Publication dates are recorded where visible; no exact web revision hashes were captured.
4. Revision 1 used compact narrative outcomes and dimension tables. Owner review rejected that depth. Revision 2 adds chronological N1–N7 sections and M1–M6 scene discussions in every dossier; synthesis follows them. Source labels resolve locally; moment references reuse evidence rather than count it twice.
5. Propose qualities from the resulting analyses, compare definitions, and review format failures. The candidate set was not supplied as a checklist before research.
6. Apply the requirements rubric as a self-review. This is not independent acceptance.

Film/source labels refer to the analyses above, not to eight independently validated traits. Source-level precision matters: Scream's general violence description does not establish the same bodily-spectacle emphasis as Terrifier's cited scenes. Temporary false escape is not equivalent to an enduring unresolved ending.

### What is useful, and what needs refinement

- **Retain for evaluation:** bodily destruction, familiar safety becoming threatening, intimate bonds under threat and genre-aware play have identifiable evidence and plausible seek/avoid meanings. Preference relevance is a hypothesis, not demonstrated by source agreement.
- **Refine:** “comic framing of danger” joins different functions: companionship, perpetrator performance and genre irony. Treat it as a broad organising concept with more specific evidence, not a reason to assume the same viewers enjoy all three.
- **Refine:** “unsecured survival” combines temporary suspense with the final state of the story. Separate phase and endpoint before using it in matching.
- **Keep provisional:** “resourceful resistance” may transfer but risks becoming generic protagonist behaviour. Test whether it describes a sustained attraction rather than mere plot necessity.
- **Do not infer absence:** this research cannot establish that a dash represents an absent feature.
- **Do not add yet:** a particular camera technique, “good acting”, “excellent writing”, or creator name as an experiential quality. A positive review is not evidence of which technical trait a viewer prefers.
- **Do not overfit overlap:** genre-aware play has strong evidence in only one selected film. It remains a plausible transferable quality; this sample cannot test its wider distribution.

The key result is that matching “gory” alone would discard the distinctions the analyses recover. Explicit content, where it occurs, its framing and reported reaction should remain distinguishable. This is an analytical finding, not an implemented recommendation rule.

## Requirements tested and changes justified

| Finding | Evidence from pilot | Change to requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole-film labels conceal temporal shape | Bone Tomahawk journey versus final confrontation; Alien's interrupted escape | Give claims and qualities a scene/phase/whole-film scope; record transitions without invented timings |
| Depiction is not experience, and experience is not liking | Scream S1 explicitly separates violence from its effect on that reviewer; Terrifier T1 distinguishes perpetrator amusement from audience address | Record depicted feature, response holder and experiential inference separately |
| Dimensions overlap naturally | A single threat to a caregiving bond can support emotion, tone and stakes in Babadook | Allow one evidence unit to support multiple claims; do not count it as multiple independent sources |
| Credible reviews contain incidental errors | Bone Tomahawk's incorrect Chicory occupation; Terrifier's inconsistent introductory metadata. The earlier alleged Buddy/Purvis error is withdrawn: it is an alias | Verify identity/narrative separately from using a review as personal-response evidence |
| Year/runtime cannot certify the cut | Terrifier's festival/distribution dates; Alien A7 includes a later-cut scene that A6 identifies explicitly | Record target and actually verified version separately; retain unknowns |
| Scene detail and endings are weaker than broad critical descriptions | Revision 1 relied heavily on one plot account per endpoint; added narrative sources improve support but leave shot-level and response gaps | Add claim-level support status and concrete evidence needed before acceptance |
| A completed table can disguise incompleteness | All seven dimensions can be written while scene, sound and version gaps remain | Separate “covered in draft” from “adequately evidenced/accepted”; do not compute completion from field count |
| Existing relationships explain sources, not future audience-wide reactions | Distinct critics and reused/syndicated material in source sets | Preserve source lineage and viewing context; no invented prevalence percentages |

These changes are added as provisional pilot refinements to the requirements. They are not evidence that a specific database schema, extraction model or numerical scoring table works.

## Evidence-depth revision findings

- **Causal narrative is a distinct requirement.** A list of qualities cannot explain why Samantha is at the jail, why Tara enters the building, why Amelia loses outside support, why quarantine fails, or why Sidney distrusts Billy. Those connections now precede synthesis.
- **Quiet scenes are evidence too.** The treatment visit, pizzeria, bedtime reading, workplace breakfast and conversation about survival rules are now examined alongside attacks and endings.
- **More detail can correct an apparent contradiction.** Bone Tomahawk B4 explains the alias used in B1; revision 1's allegation of error was itself wrong. The corrected dossier and comparison withdraw it.
- **More sources can also reveal contamination.** Alien A7 imports a later-cut scene; Terrifier T5 incorrectly frames the opening as earlier-film continuity; Scream S6 overstates sole survival. Those claims are excluded rather than adopted with a citation.
- **Source coverage is still uneven.** Additional narrative accounts improve event verification, including Babadook's ending and Alien's shuttle sequence. They do not automatically add viewer responses, precise sound evidence or identified-release verification.

The existing matrix is exploratory and refers to the revised dossiers. New possibilities such as institutional betrayal in Alien are recorded locally for later comparison; this revision does not expand or approve a taxonomy. The original eight candidates are not an exhaustive inventory of what the richer evidence might support.
