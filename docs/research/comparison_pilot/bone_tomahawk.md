## Chronological narrative

<a id="n1"></a>

### N1 — Violence before Bright Hope

Buddy and Purvis rob sleeping travellers. A wounded victim fires a shot; approaching riders drive the robbers into a burial ground. Buddy is killed there, while Purvis escapes. The opening therefore establishes violence before the rescue story begins. **Reported events: B6, opening paragraphs; escape also B3.**

<a id="n2"></a>

### N2 — Treatment becomes the reason for the rescue

In Bright Hope, Hunt shoots the suspicious Purvis when he tries to flee. Brooder brings Samantha to treat him; she remains with the patient while her injured husband Arthur is at home. By morning, Samantha, deputy Nick and Purvis have disappeared. Hunt, Chicory, Brooder and Arthur set out to recover them. Arthur's injured leg makes joining the expedition a consequential decision. **Reported events: B1, saloon/treatment/rescue passages; B3 corroborates the missing people.**

<a id="n3"></a>

### N3 — The party loses its mobility

Brooder kills two men approaching camp rather than letting Hunt question them. The party moves, but a later raid leaves them without usable horses. They must continue on foot. Arthur's leg deteriorates; a confrontation with Brooder ends with it broken. Chicory resets it, and the others leave a trail for Arthur while continuing ahead. **Reported progression: B4, campsite and leg paragraphs. The strangers' status is disputed: see M2.**

<a id="n4"></a>

### N4 — Rescuers become prisoners

At the valley, an ambush kills Brooder and leaves Hunt and Chicory captive beside Samantha and Nick. Samantha reports Purvis has been eaten. Nick is taken from confinement and killed in front of the others. Their attempted intervention has brought them into the same immediate danger as the people they sought. **Reported events: B3, cave paragraphs; B4 corroborates capture and Nick's killing.**

<a id="n5"></a>

### N5 — Two attempts to regain control

Hunt uses opium in his flask against the captors; discovery brings violent retaliation against him. Meanwhile Arthur follows the trail, kills attackers and uses their bone signalling device to lure others. His earlier separation now gives him an approach from outside the prison. **Reported actions: B3; causal reading is ours. B4 corroborates both tactics but differs on poisoning casualties.**

<a id="n6"></a>

### N6 — Rescue with someone left behind

Arthur reaches the cave and frees Samantha and Chicory. Hunt is mortally wounded and stays with a rifle, intending to deal with the remaining attackers. The departing survivors pass two mutilated pregnant women. That encounter is part of what they leave behind, not evidence that every captive is rescued. **Reported outcome: B3; the women's condition is also B5.**

<a id="n7"></a>

### N7 — Gunshots, not a witnessed final victory

The three survivors hear three shots after leaving. B4 records the sound, not a witnessed accounting of the remaining attackers. The draft therefore does not turn Hunt's intention into verified success. **Reported ending: B4, final synopsis paragraph.**

## Key moments and evidence

<a id="m1"></a>

### M1 — A medical visit creates a personal stake

**Context/action:** N2 brings Samantha away from Arthur to care for the wounded prisoner. Tallerico describes her deciding to stay because of his fever, despite Arthur's objection. **Consequence:** the disappearance turns Arthur's determination into a conflict between his own physical limits and reaching his wife. **Support:** B1 supplies the fever and objection; B4 supports the treatment arrangement. **Response/gap:** no scene-specific viewer response was obtained. The emotional importance is an inference from the relationship, not testimony that viewers felt attached.

<a id="m2"></a>

### M2 — The campsite dispute is not simply an action beat

**Context/action:** the party is already travelling into danger when Brooder shoots the approaching strangers. Bitel describes crosses rather than weapons and treats Brooder's claim that they were scouts as contestable. Culture Crypt labels them bandits. **Consequence:** N3 continues with relocation and loss of transport, but the later raid does not itself prove the dead men were scouts. **Support:** B6 supplies the moral ambiguity; B4 supplies the travel consequences. **Response/gap:** retain the disagreement; do not convert a character's suspicion into neutral fact.

<a id="m3"></a>

### M3 — Arthur's leg becomes an obstacle the group cannot ignore

**Context/action:** riding has become walking. Resetting the damaged leg and leaving Arthur behind interrupt the shared journey in N3. **Presentation/response:** Anderson singles out the leg pain and surgery as difficult to watch. That is his assessment, not a measured audience reaction. **Consequence:** the injury changes the party's composition and later rescue route; it is more than background character information. **Support:** B4 for the intervention; B5 for painful explicitness. **Gap:** no verified shot duration or sound description.

<a id="m4"></a>

### M4 — Nick's killing makes the prisoners witnesses

**Context/action:** in N4 the captives cannot intervene as Nick is scalped and split apart. **Support:** B1 identifies those acts; B3/B4 locate them within captivity; B5 independently lists scalping and bisection. These sources support the event, not every shot. **Reported response:** Huddleston describes exceptional disgust at the killing while recommending the film overall (B2). **Consequence/inference:** helpless witnessing distinguishes this from a fight in which violence might enable escape. Duration and the prisoners' individual reactions need closer verification.

<a id="m5"></a>

### M5 — Harm persists beyond the central killing

**Context/action:** N5–N6 move from resistance to departure. Anderson records a flask inserted into an abdominal wound and the blinded, amputated pregnant women. **Support:** B5's content inventory; B3 places these injuries in the cave outcome. **Consequence/inference:** the aftermath includes bodies and people the successful rescue does not restore. **Gap:** the sources do not establish the survivors' reasons for leaving the women or the scene's precise framing. Do not invent motives or treat these fictional depictions as ethnographic evidence.

<a id="m6"></a>

### M6 — A goodbye with an off-screen outcome

**Context/action:** N6–N7 separate Hunt from the escaping group. **Consequence:** the ending provides the survivors' departure and an audible indication of further conflict, leaving the final confrontation unseen in these accounts. **Reported response:** B2 finds the finale less cathartic than desired. **Support/gap:** B3/B4 broadly agree on departure and shots, but that agreement does not establish how every viewer experiences the ending. “Costly survival” remains an interpretation.

<a id="m7"></a>

### M7 — Humour can continue inside danger

B6 describes Samantha criticising the rescuers and comic attention to their failures. This is reported dialogue context, not a transcript or evidence that all viewers laugh. It qualifies the earlier journey-versus-captivity division: the intensity changes, but wit need not disappear. B2’s discomfort with tonal transitions remains compatible with that observation. The specific effect of this exchange still needs independent experiential evidence.

## Corrections and remaining gaps

Revision 1 wrongly treated B1's use of Buddy as a source error. B1 says the stranger calls himself Buddy; B4 explicitly explains Purvis's alias. That allegation is withdrawn. B2's description of Chicory as a doctor still conflicts with deputy identification elsewhere. B3 and B4 differ about how many captors the opium kills; no exact number is adopted.

Bitel offers a reading of the film's racial politics, not proof that its representation is unproblematic. Further review should examine the Professor scene, Brooder's statements and the women in the cave without adopting the sources' dehumanising vocabulary as neutral narration. Direct viewing, exact cut, sound, quieter conversations and scene timings remain unverified. This is an expanded evidence dossier, not independent acceptance.

## Re-evaluation and change log

The N2 → N3 → N4 → N6 chain explains the medical visit, loss of mobility, captivity and partial rescue. M1 supplies a relational moment and M4 a turning point. Re-reading B6 supports wit during danger, not merely a wholly comic journey followed by wholly serious horror. The evidence does not yet justify companionship as a consistent source of viewer enjoyment.

Qualify the phase boundary: humour can persist during danger. Retain M1–M6; correct the scope of the tonal synthesis rather than invent a new event.
