## Chronological narrative

<a id="n1"></a>

### N1 — Work interrupts the return home

The Nostromo's seven-person crew wakes before reaching Earth. Ordinary complaints about payment precede the explanation: a signal must be investigated under company rules. Dallas, Kane and Lambert leave to inspect its source while others remain aboard. **Reported setup: A7, awakening/contract paragraphs; workplace detail corroborated by A3.**

<a id="n2"></a>

### N2 — The expedition brings something back

Inside a derelict craft, Kane encounters eggs and is attacked by an organism that attaches to his face. Ripley interprets the signal as a warning. When the landing party returns, she refuses entry under quarantine rules, but Ash opens the hatch. The unknown organism is now inside the crew's own vessel. **Reported progression: A4, expedition/quarantine paragraphs; A6 corroborates the refusal and override.**

<a id="n3"></a>

### N3 — Medical recovery conceals a new danger

Attempts to remove the organism release corrosive blood. It later detaches and dies; Kane awakens and wants food. During the meal, a creature bursts from his chest and escapes. The crew's problem changes from treating one patient to finding a mobile threat. **Reported progression: A6, medical and meal account; chest eruption also A1/A4.**

<a id="n4"></a>

### N4 — Searching reduces the crew

Brett encounters the grown creature while retrieving the cat. Dallas then enters the ducts with a flamethrower while colleagues monitor movement, but he too is lost. The plans to locate and contain the intruder repeatedly expose individual crew members. **Reported events: A7, search paragraphs; A4 corroborates the encounters.**

<a id="n5"></a>

### N5 — The organisation has another priority

Ripley consults the computer and discovers a directive prioritising the organism over the crew. Ash attacks her; intervention exposes him as an android. His incapacitation does not supply a way to defeat the creature. **Reported events: A7, directive/Ash paragraphs; A8 corroborates the company priority and showdown.**

<a id="n6"></a>

### N6 — Destroying the ship becomes the escape plan

Parker and Lambert are killed while preparing supplies. Ripley initiates self-destruction, encounters a blocked route and cannot cancel the countdown in time. She eventually reaches the shuttle with Jones and launches before the ship explodes. **Reported sequence: A4, evacuation paragraphs. The later-cut nest discovery is excluded; see gaps.**

<a id="n7"></a>

### N7 — The escape craft also contains danger

The creature has stowed away. Ripley puts on a spacesuit, secures herself and uses decompression and the shuttle's systems to eject it. Only afterward does she settle into stasis with the cat. **Reported ending: A4, final plot paragraphs. Escape from the Nostromo and escape from the creature are separate events.**

## Key moments and evidence

<a id="m1"></a>

### M1 — The opening meal establishes a workplace

**Context/action:** the crew has awakened in N1. Graham-Dixon describes coffee, food, cigarettes and discussion of unequal bonuses, with communal living space contrasting with industrial machinery. **Consequence/inference:** these are colleagues with practical grievances before they are hunted people. **Reported response/reading:** A3 finds this everyday quality important to the horror; Thomas finds the engineers' sarcasm funny (A2). **Support:** different authors corroborate the mundane/social register, not a universal identification response. Do not merge this breakfast with Kane's later fatal meal.

<a id="m2"></a>

### M2 — A protective rule fails through another person's decision

**Context/action:** Kane is outside the hatch in N2. Dallas wants urgent treatment; Ripley objects to the risk to everyone aboard; Ash admits them. **Support:** A6's quarantine account, independently corroborated at broader level by A4. **Consequence/inference:** the creature does not physically break this barrier. The decision also becomes newly interpretable after N5. **Gap:** the later directive does not prove every early character's motive. No reported viewer reaction specifically to this exchange was obtained.

<a id="m3"></a>

### M3 — Apparent recovery is interrupted at the table

**Context/action:** N3 has restored Kane to conversation and eating. A6 describes coughing, attempts to assist him, blood through his shirt and the creature's escape. **Consequence:** treatment turns into a death and an uncontained threat. **Support:** A1/A4 corroborate the eruption; A6 supplies the local sequence. **Response/gap:** Thomas discusses bodily invasion as a fear the film engages (A2), not a collected response to each shot. Source descriptions cannot certify exact gore duration or what performers knew during filming.

<a id="m4"></a>

### M4 — A searcher depends on people who cannot reach him

**Context/action:** N4 places Dallas inside the ducts with others following the tracker. A7 records the equipment and attack; A4 supplies his plan to force the creature toward an airlock. **Consequence/inference:** distributed information does not become control of the space. **Support:** these accounts agree on the operation, while A2 discusses confinement and darkness more generally. **Gap:** the exact tracker readings, cuts and sound timing remain unverified. General claustrophobia commentary is not independent scene-by-scene corroboration.

<a id="m5"></a>

### M5 — The trusted specialist becomes another attacker

**Context/action:** N5 follows the loss of the captain with discovery of the company priority and Ash's attack. **Consequence/inference:** Ripley cannot rely on every apparent collaborator sharing the aim of survival. **Support:** A7/A8 corroborate the reveal; A3's labour interpretation offers a broader reading of institutional vulnerability. **Gap:** do not substitute that essay's interpretation for the directive itself or assume viewers recognise the betrayal at the same point. Precise assault choreography is not reconstructed here.

<a id="m6"></a>

### M6 — A successful launch is not the end of the encounter

**Context/action:** N6 destroys the larger setting, then N7 reveals the threat within the smaller refuge. **Consequence/inference:** escape must be achieved again under different constraints. Ripley's preparation, rather than another crew member's rescue, supplies her response. **Support:** A8 for the sequence of attempted ejection and final engine use; A4 corroborates the outcome. A7 stops at discovery. **Reported reading:** A2 praises Ripley's adaptation under pressure, without isolating this scene as its sole evidence. **Gap:** these accounts now corroborate the broad ending, but neither establishes this draft's exact cut or the shot/sound sequence. A8's thematic discussion is not another witness.

<a id="m6a"></a>

### M6a — Refuge contains the pursuer

N6/N7 and A4/A8 establish the shuttle discovery after destruction of the larger ship. This is temporary relief interrupted, not evidence that the final ending leaves the creature undefeated.

<a id="m6b"></a>

### M6b — Survival through practical adaptation

N7 and A4/A8 describe successive actions to remove the creature. The candidate concerns problem-solving under threat; it does not imply invulnerability or a guaranteed pleasurable audience response. A2’s admiration is broader character evaluation, not another eyewitness account of each action.

## Corrections and remaining gaps

A7 includes discovery of cocooned crew during the evacuation. A6 explicitly identifies that material as absent from the original theatrical version and restored in 2003. It is excluded from N6. A7 also loosely describes the vessel arrangement; do not use its wording to establish which vehicle is a shuttle, lander or refinery. This is a concrete version-mixing risk, not merely an unknown runtime.

The source set still concentrates on BFI commentary, with other sources improving plot coverage. Different bylines do not prove complete independence. Direct viewing of a named theatrical release, the final ejection's shot-level detail and consistent scene-level sound evidence remain needed. No screenplay or subtitles were inspected, and no later franchise explanation is imported.

## Re-evaluation and change log

Re-reading A3 reinforces the opening meal as a social baseline separate from the later meal. M2 is a decision with relational stakes; M5 changes its interpretation. The excluded later-cut nest remains excluded.

Retain M6 as a grouping; add M6a for false refuge and M6b for practical adaptation. Distinguish threat recurrence from a final unresolved ending. Retain workplace breakfast as its own moment.
