## Identity, source set and version

Damien Leone's first feature-length Terrifier, featuring David Howard Thornton as Art; not the 2011 short or sequels. Bounded to this film's narrative, without later franchise explanations. Festival year 2016 and wider US release in 2018 are distinct; “original theatrical” alone is insufficient version identification.

## Chronological narrative

<a id="n1"></a>

### N1 — The aftermath is shown first

A television interview presents a woman severely injured in a massacre. Off camera, the host mocks her appearance; the survivor attacks her. The film then turns to the earlier Halloween night. The framing makes the survivor's identity and route to that aftermath questions for the narrative. **Reported events: T2, opening synopsis.**

<a id="n2"></a>

### N2 — A public encounter becomes unwanted attention

Tara and Dawn stop for pizza after a party. Art singles Tara out; Dawn treats the encounter more playfully. He is expelled after fouling the bathroom, then returns and kills staff. The women discover a damaged tyre and call Tara's sister Victoria for a lift. **Reported events: T1, pizza synopsis; T3 for tyre and call.**

<a id="n3"></a>

### N3 — Waiting for a ride becomes separation

Tara enters a nearby building to use a toilet, meeting an exterminator and a woman who treats a doll as her child. Dawn remains outside. Art attacks them separately and sedates Tara. She wakes restrained, with Dawn held in front of her. **Reported progression: T3, building/capture paragraphs.**

<a id="n4"></a>

### N4 — Tara's resistance does not secure survival

Art makes Tara witness Dawn's killing. Tara escapes and strikes him with a board, but he responds with a gun and kills her. The apparent struggle between one pursuing killer and one resourceful survivor ends without Tara winning it. **Reported action: T5, chair/escape paragraph; interpretation of the reversal is ours.**

<a id="n5"></a>

### N5 — Victoria arrives inside the aftermath

Victoria's attempted pickup becomes a search through the building. She encounters the dead and Art's pursuit, rather than meeting her sister safely outside. Mike briefly helps her resist and seek emergency assistance, but Art kills him and injures Victoria with a vehicle before attacking her face. **Reported progression: T3, Victoria paragraph; T5 corroborates help and emergency call.**

<a id="n6"></a>

### N6 — Authorities end the attack, not the threat

When police arrive, Art shoots himself. His body goes to the morgue, where he revives and attacks the examiner. The film supplies this event without an explanation of its mechanism. **Reported ending: T3; morgue recurrence also T5. Later sequels are not evidence for an explanation within this film.**

<a id="n7"></a>

### N7 — The opening woman's identity is resolved

Victoria survives treatment and is identified as the interview subject. N1 is consequently an aftermath of this night's attacks, not a separate prior film's incident. **Reported connection: T3, final plot paragraph. T5 reaches the same identity but initially misdescribes the opening as continuity from a previous film.**

## Key moments and evidence

<a id="m1"></a>

### M1 — The pizzeria allows two responses to the same stranger

**Context/action:** in N2 the women are in a public space during a costume holiday. Williams describes Art sitting nearby, staring without ordering, with a rubbish bag beside him. Dawn's amusement contrasts with Tara's suspicion. **Consequence:** the scene gives the encounter time to remain socially ambiguous before overt attack. **Reported response:** Williams finds it funny and unnerving. **Support:** T4 supplies staging and response; T1 independently describes Art's focus on Tara. The characters' reactions are not audience testimony.

<a id="m2"></a>

### M2 — Dawn is killed for Tara to see

**Context/action:** the two friends were separated; now Tara is restrained while Dawn is suspended upside down and killed with a saw, beginning at the groin. **Support:** T1/T2 describe the explicit act; T5 establishes Tara's witness position. **Consequence/inference:** violence against one friend becomes coercion of the other. **Reported response:** Pulaski singles it out for disgust. **Gap:** detail does not establish an exact duration, uninterrupted shot or common response. Eggert discusses gendered spectacle; that is a critical interpretation, not a settled statement of intention.

<a id="m3"></a>

### M3 — The weapon change interrupts a plausible escape

**Context/action:** N4 briefly gives Tara an advantage through resistance. T5 reports repeated blows with a board followed by Art shooting her. **Consequence:** the narrative loses the woman it had followed into the building, and Victoria takes over the endangered position. **Support:** T3 corroborates escape followed by shooting; it is less specific about the board. **Response/gap:** Pulaski observes that the film lacks a conventional single final girl (T2); no independently collected reaction to the gunshot itself was found. Surprise is a hypothesis here.

<a id="m4"></a>

### M4 — An attachment object becomes leverage

**Context/action:** Art takes the doll belonging to the woman in N3. She approaches him maternally; Schell describes him responding with infant-like behaviour. **Support:** T5 for that behaviour; T2 independently identifies the doll's significance to the woman. **Consequence/inference:** her effort to recover it creates contact with someone dangerous. **Reported response:** Pulaski describes this encounter as especially tense and upsetting. No real-life diagnosis or lost-child history is established by her behaviour; his suggested backstory is not adopted as fact.

<a id="m5"></a>

### M5 — Rescue enters the same killing ground

**Context/action:** Victoria comes because her sister requested help. The outcome is recorded in N5: another helper intervenes, but both remain vulnerable. **Support:** T3/T5 agree on Mike's aid and subsequent death; detailed choreography remains thin. **Consequence/inference:** arriving later supplies no privileged safety. **Response/gap:** no scene-specific testimony supports a claim that this sequence feels tragic, exhausting or exhilarating. Eggert's general account of repetitive searching (T1) cannot answer that narrower question.

<a id="m6"></a>

### M6 — Death and survival both fail to settle the ending

**Context/action:** N6–N7 provide Art's return and Victoria's survival with lasting injury. **Presentation detail:** Schell reports electrical/audio disturbances as the body bag is opened (T5), adding detail beyond T3's compact ending. **Consequence/inference:** the police arrival and medical aftermath do not restore an ordinary safe world. **Gap:** no precise sound cue or causal account of Victoria's later violence is established. Her attack must not become a claim that disfigurement or trauma generally produces violence.

<a id="m6a"></a>

### M6a — The attacker returns after apparent death

N6 and T3/T5 locate Art’s morgue return after police intervention. This supports persistence of the threat, without explaining its mechanism. It is distinct from the survivor’s later behaviour.

<a id="m6b"></a>

### M6b — Survival is followed by another violent episode

N1/N7 and T2/T3 connect the injured survivor with the opening interview. The identity connection is supported; a causal claim that her injuries explain her violence is not. The analysis must not supply that missing explanation.

## Corrections and remaining gaps

T5's opening reference to a previous film conflicts with its own closing identification of Victoria and T3's chronology. Use it for specific corroborated events, not franchise continuity. T2's introductory dates and actor surname remain unreliable metadata. T1's displayed date differs from an aggregator date; the original register retains the observed page date.

T4 supplies a positive response to the unpleasant material; that does not erase T2's fatigue or prove most gore enthusiasts enjoy it. Building geography, transitions between minor victims, exact cut and durations remain incomplete. The dossier now makes the major causal sequence inspectable, but does not claim a complete reconstruction of every attack.

## Re-evaluation and change log

N2 → N3 → N4 → N5 preserves the delayed pickup, separation, Tara’s failed resistance and Victoria’s arrival. M1 is a sustained social encounter; M3 is a reversal. Re-reading T4 confirms contrasting character reactions and one critic’s simultaneous amusement/discomfort. T3/T5 still do not establish a supernatural explanation or a motive for Victoria’s later attack.

Split M6 into M6a (Art’s return) and M6b (Victoria/frame). Retain M6 as a historical grouping only. A single denied-safety quality conflated different aftermaths.

No independent review, direct viewing or engine evaluation occurred. Source rechecks are not new independent witnesses.
