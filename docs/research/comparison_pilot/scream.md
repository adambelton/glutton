## Chronological narrative

<a id="n1"></a>

### N1 — A conversation becomes a lethal test

Casey is preparing for a film night when repeated calls shift from conversation to threats. The caller makes her answer horror trivia while holding her boyfriend Steve outside. He is killed; Casey is attacked before she can reach safety with her returning parents. **Reported opening: S5, first three paragraphs; S3 corroborates the deaths.**

<a id="n2"></a>

### N2 — Sidney's earlier loss becomes part of the new threat

The murders occur near the anniversary of Sidney's mother's death. Her father is away, and her relationship with Billy is already strained. A threatening caller invokes her mother before attacking her. Billy arrives immediately afterward and drops a phone, making him a suspect. **Reported sequence: S7, “A Killer Story”; S1 independently describes the anniversary, absence and suspect situation.**

<a id="n3"></a>

### N3 — Apparent evidence fails to settle suspicion

Billy is arrested, but Sidney receives another threatening call. Gale disputes Sidney's identification of Cotton as her mother's killer. Sidney's missing father also becomes a suspect when calls are traced to his phone. Neither the official investigation nor the private relationships supply a stable account of whom to trust. **Reported developments: S7 for custody, the new call and Gale; S3 for the traced phone and missing father. Final sentence is synthesis.**

<a id="n4"></a>

### N4 — The party gathers people and then disperses them

School closes and Stu hosts a party. Tatum goes for beer and is killed in the garage. Randy explains horror-survival conventions while the group watches films. News of the principal's death draws many guests away, reducing the apparent protection of numbers. **Reported sequence: S3, party paragraphs; S5 corroborates Tatum's errand and encounter.**

<a id="n5"></a>

### N5 — An apparent victim is also a perpetrator

Sidney and Billy have sex; an attack apparently kills him. Sidney flees, finds Kenny killed and sees Gale crash while trying to escape. Back at the house, she locks out Randy and Stu as each accuses the other. Billy later reappears and shoots Randy, revealing his involvement with Stu. His earlier victimhood was staged. **Reported reversal: S6, red-herring and two-killer discussion; S7 supplies the escape sequence; S3 supplies the shooting.**

<a id="n6"></a>

### N6 — The explanation is part of another deception

Billy and Stu admit killing Sidney's mother. Billy blames her affair with his father for his own mother leaving. They intend to frame Sidney's father for the new murders. They wound each other to stage themselves as survivors. Gale's intervention gives Sidney an opening to resist. **Reported sequence: S3, reveal paragraph. Their account explains their stated plan; it is not an endorsement of Billy's justification.**

<a id="n7"></a>

### N7 — The anticipated last attack actually arrives

Sidney kills Stu; Billy is also brought down. Randy warns of a final return, and Sidney shoots Billy when he rises again. Dawn brings rescue and Gale's report. **Reported ending: S6, final-scare/closure discussion; S3 corroborates the survivors and reporting. This dossier describes the 1996 outcome without later sequel reinterpretations.**

## Key moments and evidence

<a id="m1"></a>

### M1 — The phone changes from social contact to control

**Context/action:** Casey's evening in N1 begins with popcorn and the possibility of casual conversation. S5 describes repeated ringing and a voice that keeps returning after she hangs up. The question-and-answer game then puts another person's life at stake. **Consequence/inference:** her knowledge is made into a coerced task rather than a freely enjoyed interest. **Support:** S5 provides the local progression; S3 corroborates the trivia premise. **Reported response:** Newman records screams at the killer's appearance in his screening (S2), not a survey of opening-scene audiences.

<a id="m2"></a>

### M2 — Suspicion affects an intimate relationship

**Context/action:** N2–N3 combine Billy's apparently incriminating arrival with evidence that seems to complicate his guilt. **Support:** S7 supplies the custody/call chronology; S6 discusses the apparent red herring. **Consequence/inference:** Sidney must make personal decisions before the identity puzzle is solved. **Gap:** neither a phone nor an apparently exculpatory call proves who performed each masked action. Do not assign every killing to Billy or Stu by inference. Ryan's interpretation of how viewers are misdirected is a reading, not testimony from everyone watching.

<a id="m3"></a>

### M3 — Tatum initially treats the encounter as a joke

**Context/action:** fetching beer separates Tatum from the party in N4. Eggert describes her joking about appearing in a sequel when confronted by the masked figure. She attempts to escape through an opening in the garage door; the killer activates it and she is killed; the precise injury is disputed in M7. **Support:** S5 for the joking; S7 for the attempted exit and door activation; S3 corroborates the fatal outcome. **Consequence/inference:** recognising genre language does not supply control over the immediate danger. **Gap:** precise movement and the door mechanics need viewing-based verification; S7 inconsistently calls the room a basement before describing the garage door; knowing the outcome is not a shot-by-shot reconstruction.

<a id="m4"></a>

### M4 — Watching a screen prevents recognition of nearby danger

**Context/action:** at the party, Randy warns the heroine of Halloween while the killer is behind his own sofa. Sidney and Kenny watch him through the planted camera's feed and also warn the screen. **Support:** S2 describes these nested viewing positions. **Consequence/inference:** the people who can see danger and the person who needs the warning are separated. **Reported reading:** Newman finds the construction amusing. **Gap:** a second detailed account of the staging and feed timing was not obtained; no exact delay is asserted.

<a id="m5"></a>

### M5 — Stated survival rules fail as literal guarantees

**Context/action:** Randy's rules include abstinence; Sidney survives despite having sex in N5. Stu's flippant departure also plays on a stated convention. **Support:** S6's rule-breaking discussion; S1 independently records the characters' attention to such rules. **Consequence/inference:** the film makes rules available for anticipation without consistently enforcing them. **Reported response:** Ebert enjoys the self-awareness and says it softened the violence for him (S1). This does not mean the depicted violence is mild or every viewer finds it funny.

<a id="m6"></a>

### M6 — The killers explain themselves, then lose control of the ending

**Context/action:** N6 turns the mystery into an attempt to manufacture evidence; N7 returns control to Sidney. Ryan describes the final killers as human and killable, while Newman identifies the last-scare convention being anticipated within the scene. **Support:** S6/S2; the confession and mutual wounding come from S3. **Consequence/inference:** explanation and practical resistance arrive together, but neither proves emotional recovery. **Gap:** Ryan's broad claims about slasher endings are not accepted as genre-wide facts; this conclusion is limited to the documented ending.

<a id="m7"></a>

### M7 — A disagreement about the garage death

S8, BBFC content advice for the 1996 film, describes decapitation through a garage door; S3 describes a fatal crushing injury. The common support is a fatal garage-door escape attempt. This run does not settle the specific injury or assume a cut difference explains it. Direct review of an identified release is needed. S8 is not a witness to viewer emotion.

## Corrections and remaining gaps

S6 calls Sidney a sole survivor in its archetype list; that conflicts with its own wider account and S3. The dossier rejects that label. Its claims about what all slasher films do, or what viewers inevitably believe, are also not empirical facts.

The film's named killer identity and a source's identification of a specific masked performer are different claims. Exact cut, individual attack attribution, garage choreography and the surveillance feed's timing remain unresolved. S2's screening report does not establish general audience prevalence; Ebert's prediction of others being horrified is not an independently collected counter-response.
