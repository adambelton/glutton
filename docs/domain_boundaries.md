## Ownership and lifecycle

Start with the action or durable state being changed. Its domain owns the mutation, permission rules and lifecycle. Identity owns accounts, credentials, sessions, guest expiry, the conduct receipt, account closure and restore replay. Community owns `CommunityUser`, public usernames and avatars, posting and conduct policy, content, endorsements, reporting, moderator roles, member blocking and suspension of Community access. Registry owns shared film knowledge. Library owns Watchlist and Watched. Ratings owns rating history, private notes, scores and profiles. Recommendations owns selection and responses. Foundation owns durable event transport and receipts, while each publisher and subscriber owns its product decisions.

**Audit example.** Accepting the Code of Conduct writes Identity's receipt, then Community provisions the profile in the same transaction. Posts and comment content point to the Community profile. Identity drives closure and replay, and the agreed Community cascades preserve protected curations and surviving replies. Check who initiates deletion and which domain owns each retained or removed record. [Community onboarding](community_niche_requests_architecture.md#community-onboarding) · [Account deletion](community_niche_requests_architecture.md#author-and-account-deletion)

**Public launch.** Reporting and content deletion are implemented. Member blocking and moderator suspension of Community access are required and await implementation. Suspension leaves the Identity account and session in place. Review the remaining Community enforcement decisions before implementing either feature. [Community launch scope](community_niche_requests_architecture.md#launch-scope) · [Implementation status](implementation_plan.md#current-work)

## Controller, service and model responsibilities

Controllers validate HTTP input and map outcomes to responses. Services load fresh entities and coordinate access checks, reads, locks and transactions. Models own domain actions and action permissions. A transition service may coordinate a change between model types, with the source model declaring which transitions it supports. Validate untrusted inputs and preserve the database constraints that enforce invariants. Check an action permission at the model and delegate the action to it. Use roles to express the behavior a collaborator needs. Use domain failures for rejected user actions. Define duplicate and retry outcomes explicitly for system workflows.

Presentation follows the same ownership. A domain presents its own facts and actions, while shared components provide generic controls. Rendering calls read operations, and application copy belongs in Rails locales. [Architecture presentation rules](architecture_brief.md#cross-domain-service-communication) · [User-facing content](../AGENTS.md#user-facing-content) · [Service naming](architecture_brief.md#service-organisation-and-naming)

**Audit example.** The curation controller parses the post and film IDs and maps an unavailable nomination to `404`. The Community orchestrator checks membership and visibility. The Library command checks the entry while holding the account lock and delegates the transition to `LibraryFilm`, which enforces its rules and emits the source fact. A stale Mark Watched action reports failure and leaves the state and event stream unchanged. ## Domain, capability and service naming

- **Who owns it?** Place an operation in the domain responsible for its outcome or lifecycle, including workflows that read from several domains. Match Rails paths to constants. Keep models, jobs, controllers and tests in their conventional layers.
- **Where does it live?** Use shallow capability directories for stable responsibilities. Keep collaborating commands, queries, calculations, AI operations, validators, value objects and assets together. A new directory needs a distinct responsibility. File count and hypothetical growth give no reason to split. Technical groupings such as Foundation’s `Publication` and `Delivery` identify different infrastructure responsibilities and transaction boundaries.
- **What is it called?** Name the responsibility, result and lifecycle. Commands, orchestrators and executable AI operations ordinarily name actions. [`ScoreRatingQualities`](../app/services/ratings/quality_scoring/score_rating_qualities.rb) applies scores. [`GenerateTasteProfile`](../app/services/ratings/taste_profiling/generate_taste_profile.rb) produces validated proposed text. [`NoteComparison`](../app/services/ratings/taste_profiling/note_comparison.rb) names a computed comparison. A `.call` method alone gives too little information to choose a name.
- **What role does each argument play?** Use `community_user` for the Community profile and `library_film` for film-specific behavior. Use `library_content` when implemented behavior spans multiple Library content types. Trust declared internal roles, leave action permissions on models and validate genuinely untrusted input at its boundary.
- **Where do failures and reactions live?** Use `Domain::Errors::Name` in the owning domain’s `errors/` directory. Transport errors such as [`TypeSafeClient::Error`](../app/services/type_safe_client.rb) stay with their component. [`Ratings::EventHandlers::Library::FilmMarkedWatched`](../app/services/ratings/event_handlers/library/film_marked_watched.rb) identifies the receiver, publisher and fact. A publisher-specific consumer such as [`Ratings::EventHandlers::ConsumeLibraryEvent`](../app/services/ratings/event_handlers/consume_library_event.rb) validates and dispatches to handlers while preserving the ordered subscription for related reactions.

**Audit example.** [`app/services/ratings/candidate_selection/next_candidates.rb`](../app/services/ratings/candidate_selection/next_candidates.rb) defines `Ratings::CandidateSelection::NextCandidates`. Ratings owns the viewer’s rating queue and admission rules. Candidate Selection groups operations for choosing rating candidates. The name tells the caller it returns the next candidates. The orchestrator obtains Registry’s vote-count ordering when Ratings determines the queue shortfall.

## Shared reads and access control

**Permission** answers which authoritative data a workflow may use. Domains can read shared records within the viewer's access and privacy rules. Community can read the shared Identity account and its account-access predicate because those facts determine whether the viewer may use Community. Community applies its own participation and visibility policy. Reading the account leaves credentials, sessions and lifecycle with Identity. Library can derive a rated marker from Ratings-owned data without storing a second value.

**Wiring** answers who arranges that permitted read. The orchestrator supplies cross-domain Library and Registry queries or their results to the consuming operation. Use a source-owned query or scope when the source owns the meaning or storage projection. A shared account lookup at an orchestration entry point can use the Identity model directly when the workflow needs its own account lock. Passing an already-loaded account is appropriate when its freshness meets the operation's contract. [Shared-read decision](domain_events.md#decision-and-boundaries) · [Read and wiring rules](architecture_brief.md#cross-domain-service-communication)

**Audit example.** [`PostFilmDebrief#require_view_access!`](../app/models/community/post_film_debrief.rb) applies Community’s post access rules and yields the viewer account ID and the debrief’s film ID. [`ReadPost`](../app/services/community/posts/read_post.rb) calls [`BrowseWatchedFilms.entry`](../app/services/library/collection_management/browse_watched_films.rb) with those IDs and returns the result through the block. Library owns what qualifies as a Watched entry. Community requires the entry for visibility. For locked reads the Library lookup takes the entry lock before Community locks the post. Check the visibility gate on direct posts, comment submissions and report targets. [Film Debrief scope](community_niche_requests_architecture.md#community-board-and-post-types)

## Orchestration and dependency injection

When a workflow composes multiple services, put its orchestrator in the domain that owns the outcome. Each collaborator retains its own queries, rules and transaction responsibilities. The orchestrator can supply a value it already has when that value meets the consumer’s freshness requirements. When a collaborator determines the lookup inputs or needs a read at a specific point under lock, it yields those inputs. The orchestrator calls the owning query and returns its result through the block. The consuming operation applies its own rule. The transaction owner acquires any required account lock directly. An independently meaningful collaborator can be supplied as an operation when the caller delegates that responsibility, as Registry does with `LoadFilms` and its single-film loader. [Lock, read and collaborator rules](architecture_brief.md#required-locks-cross-domain-reads-and-collaborators)

**Ratings example.** [`ChooseInitialRatingFilms`](../app/services/ratings/candidate_selection/choose_initial_rating_films.rb) and [`NextCandidates`](../app/services/ratings/candidate_selection/next_candidates.rb) each acquire the account lock and hold it through the queue decision and write. [`InitialRatingFilmSelection`](../app/services/ratings/candidate_selection/initial_rating_film_selection.rb) yields deduplicated IDs for Registry validation. [`ReplenishCandidateQueue`](../app/services/ratings/candidate_selection/replenish_candidate_queue.rb) yields the exclusion IDs and batch shortfall for Registry ordering. The Ratings operations own eligibility and queue admission.

**Audit example.** [`ManageCurationFilmInLibrary`](../app/services/community/curations/manage_curation_film_in_library.rb) acquires the locks it promises Library. Its private `with_nomination` method yields the locked viewer and film ID after checking the nomination. Here the block selects between Library’s Add to Watchlist and Mark Watched commands. The Debrief example uses its block to get data for a Community decision. ## Transactions, locks and freshness

Establish freshness at the boundary that needs it. Review which transaction owns the write, which rows it locks and in what order, and whether the lock survives through the operation. A loaded Rails association or a true `persisted?` result cannot establish that the row still exists or that this caller holds its lock. Community's profile-write operation locks the account first, then loads its Community profile through that freshly locked account. The caller owns the permission and missing-profile outcome. For Film Debrief writes, the later locks are Watched entry and debrief, in that order.

- **When reuse is valid.** The caller holds the same account lock through the callee's write in the enclosing transaction. On a curation shortcut, Community locks the account, then the curation, checks nomination visibility, and hands that account to Library. Library keeps its activity check and savepoint. The standalone Library entry point loads and locks the account identified by its `user_id:` argument. Both paths use the same Library mutation for the chosen action.
- **When reuse improves the design.** Recommendation acceptance already holds the response owner’s account lock and passes that account to Foundation for the outbox write. Library and Ratings derive event owners from `LibraryFilm#user_id` and `Rating#rating_film.user_id`. They keep ID-based publication so their models have one authoritative owner and no second account argument.
- **When freshness starts again.** Dispatch and delivery begin later transactions. Each locks and checks the account for its own work. Closure can commit between publication, dispatch and delivery.

## Asynchronous coordination

A member clicking Add to Watchlist on a curation explicitly requests a Library change. Community checks account access and nomination visibility, then calls Library’s command. Library owns the transition and publishes the resulting fact. A rejected user action reports the agreed failure and emits no fact. [Curation Library actions](community_niche_requests_architecture.md#library-actions-on-nominated-films) · [Action outcomes](../AGENTS.md#action-outcomes-and-idempotency)

Use durable events when one product domain needs to react to a committed fact from another. The publisher commits its source change and outbox append together. Foundation wakes dispatch after the outer commit and recovers through polling. Delivery is at least once. Each subscriber commits its own effects and receipt together. Subscribers preserve order within their streams, suppress duplicate effects and retry handler failures. A failed predecessor holds back later events in that subscriber’s stream while unrelated streams continue.

Agree the event’s meaning, minimal payload, schema version and compatibility rules, ordering, retries, duplicate and failure outcomes, bootstrap and retention before implementation. Keep publisher and subscriber verification separate and owned by those domains. [Event contract](domain_events.md#delivery-contract-and-failure-handling) · [Operations and recovery](domain_event_operations.md#inspection-and-recovery) · [Retention](domain_event_operations.md#retention-and-boundaries)

**Audit example.** `RecommendationAccepted` v1 means the viewer accepted the recommendation. Its payload is the internal `user_id`, `film_id` and `recommendation_film_id`. The event is ordered in Recommendations’ per-viewer, per-film stream. Verify Recommendations saves the response and exact event atomically, rolls both back on failure and wakes dispatch only after commit. Library later adds a Watchlist entry if no entry exists, with an existing entry handled as a no-op. Verify Library’s effect and receipt together, safe duplicate delivery and the agreed missing or inactive account and missing film outcomes. [Recommendation event contract](domain_events.md#rating-and-recommendation-events)

## AI workflows and verification

The outcome's domain owns AI orchestration, evidence, assessment and application rules. The configured client owns authentication, transport, timeout and retry defaults. Assessments own product prompts or questions, response schemas, interpretation and domain failures. Reuse the existing client where it supports the call. A separate adapter requires a capability the client lacks and owner agreement. Call external providers outside write locks. Before application, lock and recheck versions and source evidence. The aggregate owns applicability and application. Evaluation and result storage follow the owning workflow's agreed retention and atomicity. Record the evidence and versions needed to explain a result, and apply the workflow's trace privacy rules. [Evidence and configuration](ai_model_services_design.md#evidence-and-configuration) · [Tracing and privacy](ai_model_services_design.md#tracing-and-privacy)

**Audit example.** Taste-profile refresh captures the current notes, generates the assessment outside locks, then rechecks the notes and current revision before applying. Tests stub the configured client and exercise the public workflow, including stale application and retention of the prior result. [AI roles and lifecycle](ai_model_services_design.md#roles) · [AI verification](ai_model_services_design.md#verification) · [Ratings profile consistency](ratings_scoring_design.md#consistency-check-before-profile-work)

For all domains, test public entry points and observable outcomes, including state, ownership, response, event and rollback contracts. Use the model's named mutations for state-changing setup. Get the CSRF token and submitted values from the rendered form. Open a stale form before arranging the competing write, and test distinct failure stages separately. Verify JavaScript-dependent behavior in the browser. A one-off SQL trace can substantiate an intentional lock change during review. Prefer persistence, outcome and concurrency assertions in the maintained suite. [Testing conventions](../AGENTS.md#testing-conventions) · [Development verification](development.md#setup-and-verification)

## Open decisions

- **Username moderation.** Community owns the username. Reporting, enforcement and protection of a replacement username need an agreed policy. A generic fallback label remains a proposal. [Community reports and decisions](community_niche_requests_architecture.md#reports-and-decisions)
- **Member blocking enforcement.** Confirm how blocks affect direct replies, visibility in shared threads, attribution, and stale requests. [Member blocking](community_niche_requests_architecture.md#member-blocking)
- **Community suspension enforcement.** Agree which Community reads and writes are gated, how existing sessions and stale forms pick up the decision, how prior contributions are treated, and how suspension ends. [Community suspension](community_niche_requests_architecture.md#community-suspension)
- **Reversible hiding and restoration.** Decide whether to support these alongside the implemented deletion options. [Reports and decisions](community_niche_requests_architecture.md#reports-and-decisions)
- **Future events and retention.** Accepted-quality reassessment, new publishers or subscriptions, and active-owner event compaction require their own contracts and decisions. [Future contracts](domain_events.md#future-contracts) · [Retention](domain_event_operations.md#retention-and-boundaries)
